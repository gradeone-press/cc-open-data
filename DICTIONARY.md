## The one thing to read before you judge the coverage

`sources.csv` carries a locator on 57 of 200 rows. Another 83 record why no locator exists. The remaining 60 have neither.

**Those 60 rows are grade 4 and 5, and only grade 4 and 5.** Grade 4 is an aggregator — a salary site built from what people said about themselves. Grade 5 is a company describing the thing it sells. They are in the bibliography because the book argues with them, not because it rests on them, and several are not documents at all: the publisher column says so in words like "widely circulated industry claim".

Coverage of a locator is therefore a function of grade, by design. Here it is, per grade:

| grade | rows | with a locator | with a stated reason | date read |
| ----- | ---: | -------------: | -------------------: | --------: |
| 1 | 75 | 42 (56%) | 33 | 75 (100%) |
| 2 | 33 | 14 (42%) | 19 | 6 (18%) |
| 3 | 29 | 1 (3%) | 28 | 14 (48%) |
| 4 | 50 | 0 (0%) | 3 | 1 (2%) |
| 5 | 13 | 0 (0%) | 0 | 0 (0%) |

`retrieved` follows the same logic: you record the day you read something you can go and read. It is close to complete at grade 1 and close to empty at grade 5.

### A warning about `closeable`

It reads `yes` on the rows where a published source exists and nobody has compiled it, and is **empty on the other 116**. Empty means no. It does not mean unknown. The book decides this for every gap; the blank is a formatting choice in the export rather than an absence of judgement, and it should be written out as `no` in a future edition.
